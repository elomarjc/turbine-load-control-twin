🌬️ Wind Turbine Load Control & Aero-Elastic Twin

🎯 The Industrial Problem Solved

15 MW offshore wind turbines use massive 118-meter flexible composite blades. Because wind speed increases with altitude (atmospheric wind shear), blades experience significantly higher aerodynamic thrust at the top of their rotation (268 m) than at the bottom (32 m). This creates asymmetric 1P cyclic bending moments on the rotor hub and main bearings, accelerating structural fatigue and causing millions in bearing replacements.

💡 How This Digital Twin Solves It

Applies the Coleman Multi-Blade Coordinate (MBC) transformation to project rotating blade root moments (My1, My2, My3) into non-rotating tilt (Md) and yaw (Mq) axes. Individual Pitch Control (IPC) regulates separate cyclic pitch angles (Δβ_i) for each blade, eliminating structural fatigue while maintaining steady 12.55 MW electrical power.

📊 Telemetry & Control Glossary

  • Coleman (d-q) Transform: Mathematical multi-blade coordinate transformation that converts rotating blade-frame flapwise moments into stationary orthogonal tilt (Md) and yaw (Mq) reference frames.
  • 1P Cyclic Loading: Once-per-revolution aerodynamic force variations caused by the vertical wind shear gradient across the rotor swept area.
  • Rainflow Cycle Counting: Standard ASTM fatigue damage calculation algorithm that converts variable-amplitude stress histories into equivalent fatigue cycles.
